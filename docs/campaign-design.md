## The game in one minute

You lead a government through ten annual budgets. Households want secure incomes and affordable homes. Businesses want customers, finance and workable investment terms. You decide how much to redistribute, what to build, and whether public enterprises or private firms should deliver it. Parliament can demand changes; lenders can refuse a deal; a project can consume money for years before helping anyone.

Each year you read a short briefing, assemble a funded budget and up to two major reforms, negotiate their terms, then advance time. You see actual jobs, household incomes, completed assets, unpaid obligations and the reasons behind them. The next year begins with what you built and what you still owe. At the end, the game judges your chosen mandate and exposes its costs. Replay lets you try a different economic approach under the same external conditions.

The strategic challenge is **immediate relief versus durable capacity, public control versus delegated decisions, and promises versus feasible delivery**. There is no universally correct public/private mix and no score awarded merely for choosing either.

## Recommended campaign defaults

| Choice | Recommendation | Reason and alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First release | One complete UK campaign; other five nations follow later | A complete campaign is a substantial implementation. Alternative: choose another researched nation before authoring its rules. |
| Opening and duration | 1 January 2010; ten annual turns, 2010–2019 inclusive, final assessment at the start of 2020 | Allows investment and debt to outlast individual decisions. This replaces the older 15-turn recommendation for this proposed release. Alternative: five turns with less long-term development. |
| Policy scope | Income and employment; productive enterprises; housing delivery | These compete for money, labour, credit and political agreement. Alternative: a different connected set, rather than silently reducing the game to a single housing exercise. |
| Difficulty | One standard scenario initially; no selectable difficulty multipliers | Establish a coherent economy before tuning difficulty variants. |
| History | Researched opening institutions with a counterfactual future | No forced replay of later governments, legislation or historical outcomes. |
| Player identity | The executive office across administrations | Leadership changes preserve assets, contracts and obligations. No personal politician statistics or election minigame. |
| Presentation | Portrait-first phone strategy interface: briefing, policies, commitments, report | Charts and actor detail support decisions; no city-building map or real-time controls. |
| Actors | Real institutional names; fictional composite businesses, lenders and negotiating representatives | Avoid invented behavior attributed to real people or firms. Composites and behavioral assumptions are labeled. |
| Release | Playable web link, reliable local saves, export/import, campaign ending and replay | These are parts of the proposed finished release, not a prototype milestone offered as the deliverable. |

The UK recommendation is a design judgment, not a claim that the research selected it. The [UK profile]({{ '/nation-uk-2010/' | relative_url }}) supplies the historical basis: parliamentary fiscal authority, an operationally independent monetary committee, mixed ownership and distinct devolved responsibilities. Its source register and remaining gaps remain authoritative. No additional nation survey is needed to review this recommendation.

**Geographic boundary:** taxes, national finances and production use their correctly sourced UK or applicable statutory coverage; detailed housing delivery is in England. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland retain separate institutions and budget accounts. The interface must say “England housing,” never present those delivery results as UK-wide. Devolved service use is an autonomous background component, not an English minister's control panel. Other services remain funded obligations, rather than disappearing from the budget because their management is outside the catalogue.

**Historical boundary:** future institutions change only through represented reforms. This is explicitly a counterfactual continuation of the opening framework, not an assertion that UK law remained unchanged until 2019. Later observed domestic GDP, employment or interest rates cannot be pasted in as outcomes of player policy. Carbon-emissions and climate-change mechanics remain entirely excluded.

## Starting a campaign

The start screen offers New campaign and Resume. A new campaign shows its nation, dates, scope and simplifications, then asks the player to select one mandate:

| Mandate | Primary result to improve | Tradeoff the player must watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Household security | Real disposable income after housing costs for the opening lowest-income fifth | Transfers can improve current income while consuming funds needed for investment. |
| Productive employment | Employment among working-age residents | Public orders, training and investment need funding and may displace other work. |
| Affordable homes | Reduce England's measured number of households lacking suitable affordable housing | Construction, tenure, eligibility and household purchasing power all matter; announced projects count for nothing until usable. |

All three use the same economy and policy catalogue. The mandate changes assessment, not the laws of economics, available evidence or actor intelligence. The player sees the exact metric definition, baseline and success test before confirming.

The first briefing presents inherited conditions and obligations: current revenue and spending, debt coming due, household groups under pressure, available capacity and projects already underway. The player can inspect evidence, but no quiz or paper walkthrough stands between them and the first decision.

## What the player does in a year

| Step | Player action | What the game changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Briefing | Read three priority problems; inspect households, firms or commitments | Nothing changes from reading. The briefing separates due obligations from optional proposals. |
| 2. Budget | Keep or amend tax/transfer schedules, routine operating allocations and financing | A draft forecasts funding gaps and bottlenecks. It cannot spend money or cancel contracts. |
| 3. Initiatives | Draft up to two structural changes from the catalogue | Each has an authority route, funding, beneficiaries, resource requirements and stages. |
| 4. Bargaining | Submit; accept a counteroffer, revise terms, or withdraw | Submission consumes that initiative's slot. Revision within the same objective does not consume another; replacing it with a different objective does. |
| 5. Commitment | Confirm the final package after approvals and financing | Only authorized commitments enter the schedule. New terms are shown alongside the original offer. |
| 6. Resolution | Press Advance year and answer any material delivery encounter | The resolver executes obligations and actor decisions. It pauses at saved decisions rather than resolving a hidden default. |
| 7. Report | Inspect results and choose Continue | Delivered output, distribution, finance and unfinished commitments become next year's opening state. |

**The annual budget is not a third unlimited reform.** Adjusting a rate or funding an existing service within its established remit belongs to the budget. Changing eligibility architecture, creating a program, changing ownership or imposing a new regulatory regime uses an initiative. Both routes still require the actual institutional authorization. No initiative is required merely to honor an existing contract.

Routine administration and ordinary loan decisions resolve automatically and appear in the report. Encounters interrupt only when a decision changes the player's promised terms, requires authority, or presents a material failure. The game has no countdown and no real-time skill requirement.

“Keep current policy” is always a valid draft, although expiring authority or financing may still require a decision. Doing nothing does not freeze population, obligations or the economy.

## The policy catalogue

These are proposed launch actions, not findings that every route is already legally or quantitatively specified. Each route needs the policy-specific authority record in the completion checklist. Every form shows current expenditure, future expenditure, contingent exposure and displaced spending separately.

| Package | Player controls | Counterparty or limit | Persistent consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Taxes and household support | Select a supported tax schedule change, benefit level and eligible group; fund processing capacity | Parliament and the appropriate administering body; coverage follows the scheme's jurisdiction | Disposable income and public receipts change through the actual tax/benefit calculation; administration may delay payment. |
| Employment and training | Choose eligible workers, funded training places, employer participation and duration | Providers need instructors and places; employers need vacancies or viable expansion | Trainees are not immediately additional skilled workers; completed training changes eligible labour supply without guaranteeing a job. |
| Conditional business investment | Offer grants, procurement contracts or loan guarantees; specify milestones, expiry and repayment/clawback terms | Firms may reject the return or delivery obligations; competition/subsidy rules apply | Capacity arrives after completion; guarantees can become payments; unused offers produce no investment. |
| Public enterprise investment | Fund an existing public enterprise or propose a consent-based acquisition; set service/output obligations and a financial budget | Shareholder powers, transaction authority, valuation, seller consent and management capacity | Assets and liabilities transfer explicitly; managers allocate resources under the mandate and still face costs. |
| Enterprise sale or partnership | Select assets/stake, conditions and eligible bids; retain or transfer defined control | A buyer must fund the price and accept enforceable terms | One-time receipts trade against future income/control; obligations do not vanish. |
| England housing delivery | Commission public/social homes or contract private delivery; choose location, tenure, eligibility and capital commitment | Local bodies, land, permission, builders, finance and infrastructure readiness | Homes are usable only after completion; rents, maintenance and financing continue afterward. |
| Delivery and market rules | Propose enforceable contract standards, procurement access or permit-processing resources | Relevant legislature, regulator or local authority; courts review actual legal issues | Administration costs money; enforcement affects compliance, rather than granting an abstract efficiency bonus. |

Markets and planning are choices inside these packages. A public enterprise can receive a production mandate within its actual control. A private firm responds to prices, contracts and regulation. There is no “planned economy” switch that grants command over every private balance sheet. Expanding compulsory allocation powers would require a separately specified reform; it is outside this launch catalogue.

Trade enters through export orders, imported inputs and commercial counterparties. The UK player can pursue lawful export support and negotiate commercial procurement terms. They cannot set a unilateral national external tariff where the researched European authority applies. A full treaty-negotiation system is beyond this first campaign.

## Why the actors respond

Actor choices use feasible actions and declared priorities, not arbitrary agree/disagree dice. The first release should use deterministic decisions; versioned external events provide uncertainty. Where exact values remain unknown, the interface shows a range or an explicit unknown, not an invented numerical probability.

| Actor | Decision rule proposed for this campaign | How the player can respond |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legislative negotiating blocs | Reject a package that violates a recorded red line; otherwise support if its required conditions are met. Voting weights follow the scenario's researched chamber composition; interests/red lines are labeled design assumptions. | Change funding, targeting, oversight or timing. A bloc's vote is not purchasable with an abstract currency. |
| Courts/review bodies | Evaluate an authored legal condition when a relevant challenge is triggered; remedies follow the researched route. No generic random veto or judicial popularity meter. | Cure the defect, seek a lawful alternative, or abandon the affected action. A delay preserves associated carrying costs. |
| Private firms | Reject infeasible production; among financeable projects prefer the highest expected net present value under their stated forecast; invest only when that value is positive. Stable actor ID breaks exact ties. | Alter contracts, prices or risk-sharing. Expectations can be wrong; forecasts use known information only. |
| Public enterprise managers | First satisfy legally binding service commitments; then allocate the authorized budget to the player's ranked feasible projects. Report unmet mandates. | Change the mandate, budget or governance through the proper authority. Public ownership supplies neither free inputs nor automatic competence. |
| Banks | Reject lending that fails the modeled capital, liquidity, collateral or repayment constraints; rank remaining loans by expected net return. Existing obligations and repricing dates persist. | Offer a lawful guarantee or change the project. A guarantee transfers specified losses; it does not compel lending. |
| Delivery bodies | Accept feasible funded schedules; request revised terms when land, staff, procurement or permissions prevent delivery | Fund capacity, phase delivery, change scope or cancel subject to termination costs. |
| Household groups | Pay unavoidable obligations, buy their essential basket subject to income/credit, then allocate discretionary consumption and saving under the published rule | Change taxes, transfers, access, supply or employment conditions. Distribution matters separately from average output. |
| Monetary committee | Independently apply its published model rule to lagged inflation/activity information | Observe and adjust fiscal decisions; there is no cabinet interest-rate slider. The reaction function is a model assumption requiring parameters. |

The player sees motives and constraints relevant to the offer. They do not see private future information. All comparative policies use the same actor rules; ownership changes objectives and rights, not the physical accounting.

## What the simulation actually resolves

The [campaign rule specification]({{ '/simulation/' | relative_url }}#campaign-rule-specification) owns calculation order and equations. The proposed representation is:

- Household groups by income, employment and tenure, with population weights and financial positions. Group totals must reconcile; cross-classifications cannot double-count people.
- Production in construction, manufacturing and an aggregate remaining domestic sector. Each has capacity, labour, inputs, orders, stocks and costs. Visible firms sit inside sector totals rather than being added to them twice.
- Projects with preparation, construction/equipment, commissioning and operation stages. Every stage requires a bill of resources and financing; a completed asset starts providing service from the following modeled period.
- Treasury accounts, contractual debt cohorts and appropriations; banks and their matching loans/deposits; an explicit external counterpart for imports, exports and finance.
- A limited set of independently acting institutions and autonomous devolved accounts. Services outside the catalogue retain funding and resource requirements through declared background rules.

A policy changes a contract, transfer, capacity, constraint or decision rule. Those changes alter spending and delivery; effects propagate to income, demand, employment, prices and receipts. No policy writes “GDP +2” into state. GDP, employment and distribution are derived from the represented transactions and populations.

## Progress, events and recovery

Progress comes from the player's previous actions. A completed project adds capacity; an operating enterprise generates costs and receipts; skills become available after training; maturing debt has to be repaid or refinanced. Policy options depend on relevant authority and feasibility, not experience points or an arbitrary technology tree.

External event records specify the year, information revealed, affected input and permitted responses. Proposed event families are changes in export demand, imported input availability and external financing conditions. Numerical paths need explicit provenance or a labeled scenario assumption. Events cannot prescribe domestic outcomes such as “unemployment rises by three points.” A common event path is fixed when the campaign starts and reused for comparison.

Domestic failures arise from state: a builder fails to obtain credit, a project lacks materials, an enterprise cannot meet payroll, or a guarantee is called. The resulting choices are concrete: restructure, provide authorized support, delay, reduce scope or allow closure through the applicable route. A headline is generated from the failure, not randomly pasted over a healthy enterprise.

**Political continuity recommendation:** a package defeat normally rejects that package. It does not automatically dissolve government. A genuine confidence/authority crisis follows the researched route and presents a successor mandate. The player continues as the executive office, accepting an explicitly negotiated governing program while retaining outstanding obligations. No prediction of voter choices or automatic historical election winner is claimed. Confidence triggers, fallback spending authority and the abstract successor selection must be finalized in the authority artifact before coding this branch.

There is no instant game-over for low GDP or high debt. If funding is unavailable, unsupported commitments cannot be made and the player must choose a feasible recovery budget. Distress can make the mandate fail; it cannot be escaped by deleting liabilities. At any time the player may end the campaign early and receive an incomplete-term assessment.

## Campaign ending and success

After resolving 2019, the final report covers the entire term and all outstanding commitments. A capital project that will finish later is shown as unfinished; its promised benefits are not counted. Contractual future costs, asset values and debt maturities remain visible. Selling an asset in the final year is not recurring income.

**Recommended success rule:** compare the final three-year average of the mandate metric with both its opening baseline and a no-policy-change comparison using the same scenario and external events. For Household security and Productive employment, the average must be strictly higher than both; for Affordable homes, strictly lower than both. Ties are “not achieved.” The exact population, price basis and affordability definition must be published before release; they cannot change during a campaign.

Use the opening lowest-income fifth as a fixed cohort for the household-security objective, alongside a separately reported current distribution. Employment uses the defined working-age population, not only the labour force. Housing uses England households and a fixed disclosed suitability/affordability definition. Missing comparable observations block scenario release rather than becoming zero.

A campaign earns **Mandate achieved** only if that improvement test passes and no legally due modeled public payment remains unpaid at the final close. Otherwise show **Mandate not achieved** and the specific failed tests. This solvency guard is a proposed game rule, not an economic theorem. Report guarantees and future liabilities even when the guard passes. Do not award a hidden aggregate morality or ideology score.

The comparison preserves opening policy schedules and applies their defined expiry rules; autonomous actors and existing projects still evolve. It is a modeled comparison, not “what history would certainly have done.” The player can inspect actual outcomes without accepting the model's causal accuracy.

After the ending, offer Review decisions, Replay the same conditions, or Start a different mandate. There is no requirement to unlock nations by winning. Other nations appear as research links until actually implemented, not selectable empty campaigns.

## What appears on the phone

The screen should feel like a working government brief: readable cards, compact trends and clearly labeled decisions. A fixed header shows nation, year, phase and save status. Bottom navigation opens Briefing, Policies, Commitments and Report. Actor conversations use short offers and term comparisons, not an endless chat interface.

A typical sequence is: open “Investment is stalled” → inspect a firm's financing constraint → draft a conditional contract → see which funding must move → receive a lender's terms → accept or revise → see the new obligation → advance → inspect whether production actually increased and who received the income. This describes intended interaction, not a completed playthrough or a synthetic simulation result.

The [phone interface chapter]({{ '/interface/' | relative_url }}#campaign-screen-sequence) owns controls and error behavior. Every irreversible confirmation summarizes money, authority, future obligations and the next phase. Every report highlight offers “Why?” with the actual rule and transaction trail. Detail is optional; basic consequences are not hidden behind it.

## Realism and its limits

Historical institutions, period-specific authority and reconciled starting data provide the setting. Contractual accounting, resource limits and actor incentives make decisions consequential. Behavioral equations, actor groupings, annual scheduling, initiative limits and success tests are deliberate simplifications, labeled as such.

The first release does not simulate every person, financial security, service, election or foreign government. Its price, investment and monetary responses must be documented and sensitivity-tested. A convincing narrative is not proof of calibrated economics. No confidence percentage or predicted national outcome can be claimed without supporting evidence.

## From this design to actual code

This proposal resolves the recommended experience and scope for review. It does **not** close every numerical or legal implementation dependency. The remaining work is a finite set of artifacts for this campaign, rather than another broad nation-research phase:

| Artifact | Exact remaining content | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Opening pack | Reconciled 2009/January-2010 household, sector, bank and public accounts; price bases; debt maturities; rights and vintage records | Campaign initialization and baseline reports |
| B. Authority routes | For each launch package: proposing/approving/implementing body, applicable 2010 law, votes/conditions, deadlines, failure remedy; include devolution, subsidy review and confidence/continuity | Proposal validation, negotiations and legally meaningful failure branches |
| C. Behavioral register | Complete the rule inventory below with price/wage adjustment, consumption, investment, loan-risk, monetary, foreign-demand and delivery inputs; distinguish evidence from design assumptions | Deterministic economic resolution without hidden constants |
| D. Independent fixtures | Reconciled no-change run, public-delivery alternative and private-contract alternative; shortages, financing refusal, project cancellation and final assessment | Meaningful automated economic tests |
| E. Content and release contract | Authored policy forms/encounters, source labels, save versions, phone browser coverage and production build choices | A usable and recoverable game release |

**Coding milestones, once their dependencies and implementation scope are authorized:**

1. Implement campaign initialization, annual budget, authority checks and durable saves using A/B and the relevant accounting fixtures. Deliver a tested production foundation that stays in the game.
2. Implement all three connected policy areas, actor decisions and resource/finance settlement against C/D. Integrate the actual phone decision and report screens as each behavior works.
3. Implement ten-turn progression, recovery, final assessment and comparable replay. Verify different strategies through complete campaigns.
4. Complete phone accessibility, save recovery, economic sensitivity and live release checks. Publish the finished campaign while preserving this documentation site.

Internal implementation increments are not a substitute deliverable for the game. No prototype review or aborted paper exercise is required. Do not postpone all coding until unrelated nations, optional features or perfect predictive calibration are available; likewise, do not implement a dependent numerical rule using fabricated values.
