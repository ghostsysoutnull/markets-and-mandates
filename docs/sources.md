## Current coverage

These primary institutional references support the initial explanations of central banking and money creation. They do not establish a calibrated game model, validate policy-effect sizes, or constitute complete national profiles.

Institutions explain their own frameworks and analysis. Broader research, historical evidence, and contrasting scholarly findings will be needed when choosing model assumptions.

## Monetary institutions and mechanisms

- **Bank of England — [About a rate of (general) interest: how monetary policy transmits](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2024/2024/about-a-rate-of-general-interest-how-monetary-policy-transmits)** (2024). Supports the explanation that interest rates affect activity and inflation through several channels, with effects shaped by economic structure.
- **Bank of England — [Money creation in the modern economy](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy)** (2014). Supports the distinction between commercial-bank deposit creation and simplified accounts of banks merely lending pre-existing deposits.
- **European Central Bank — [ECB, ESCB and the Eurosystem](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/orga/escb/html/index.bs.html)**. Explains the institutions responsible for euro-area monetary policy. Supports Germany's shared monetary-policy constraint in the modern scenario.
- **European Central Bank — [Independence](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/our-values/independence/html/index.en.html)**. Describes the ECB's institutional independence and restrictions on government instructions and financing.
- **International Monetary Fund — [Central Bank Independence: Why It's Needed and How to Protect It](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2024/06/17/sp061424-central-bank-independence)** (2024). Background on the dimensions of independence; represents the institution's analysis and policy perspective.

## Research still required

- Dated starting profiles for every selected nation, including period-specific monetary mandates and legal powers.
- Comparable economic, fiscal, sectoral, demographic, and distributional data.
- Evidence on ownership reforms, competition, public provision, planning systems, and institutional capacity.
- Policy lags, uncertainty, distributional effects, and variation across national circumstances.
- Historical shocks and a rule for separating external events from policy-dependent developments.
- Separate research for any 1980s scenario.

For each profile or mechanism, record the observation period, definitions, source, simplification, and uncertainty. Do not treat a contemporary institutional page as sufficient evidence of its exact 2010 rules.
