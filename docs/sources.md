## Current coverage

These primary institutional references support the explanations of central banking, money creation, banking resilience, competition assessment, and trade institutions. They do not establish a calibrated game model, validate policy-effect sizes, or constitute complete national profiles.

Institutions explain their own frameworks and analysis. Broader research, historical evidence, and contrasting scholarly findings will be needed when choosing model assumptions.

## Monetary institutions and mechanisms

- **Bank of England — [About a rate of (general) interest: how monetary policy transmits](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2024/2024/about-a-rate-of-general-interest-how-monetary-policy-transmits)** (2024). Supports the explanation that interest rates affect activity and inflation through several channels, with effects shaped by economic structure.
- **Bank of England — [Money creation in the modern economy](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy)** (2014). Supports the distinction between commercial-bank deposit creation and simplified accounts of banks merely lending pre-existing deposits.
- **European Central Bank — [ECB, ESCB and the Eurosystem](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/orga/escb/html/index.bs.html)**. Explains the institutions responsible for euro-area monetary policy. Supports Germany's shared monetary-policy constraint in the modern scenario.
- **European Central Bank — [Independence](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/our-values/independence/html/index.en.html)**. Describes the ECB's institutional independence and restrictions on government instructions and financing.
- **International Monetary Fund — [Central Bank Independence: Why It's Needed and How to Protect It](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2024/06/17/sp061424-central-bank-independence)** (2024). Background on the dimensions of independence; represents the institution's analysis and policy perspective.

## Banks and regulation

- **Bank of England — [Bank capital and liquidity](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2013/q3/bank-capital-and-liquidity)** (2013). Explains distinct banking risks and the relationship between capital and liquidity. Supports keeping loan losses and payment pressures distinguishable in NPC behavior.
- **Bank for International Settlements — [Basel Framework](https://www.bis.org/committees/bcbs/basel-framework)**. Reference for international bank prudential standards, including capital, liquidity, supervision, and disclosure. The framework is not a substitute for checking a country's actual rules and their historical implementation.
- **Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development — [Competition assessment](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/competitive-and-fair-markets/competition-assessment.html)**. A framework for examining how regulation affects competition and entry. Supports the questions asked in the regulation chapter, not predetermined effect sizes.

## Trade and shared authority

- **World Trade Organization — [Tariffs](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/tariffs_e/tariffs_e.htm)**. Background on tariff commitments and bindings. Country schedules, exceptions, and the relevant year still need research.
- **World Trade Organization — [Anti-dumping, subsidies, safeguards: contingencies, etc.](https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/whatis_e/tif_e/agrm8_e.htm)**. Introduces conditions around trade remedies and subsidies. Supports representing legal constraints rather than universal permission or prohibition.
- **Council of the European Union — [Trade Policy Committee](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/council-eu/search-the-list-of-council-preparatory-bodies/trade-policy-committee/)**. Describes the committee's role in assisting Commission negotiations on behalf of the EU. Supports distinguishing national from shared trade-policy authority.

## Status of the new examples

Corporate strategies, negotiations, enforcement sequences, and the four worked scenarios are proposed game designs. They are not case studies extracted from these references. Their outcomes are conditional illustrations, and no numerical calibration has been performed.

The executive role, legislative blocs, judicial encounters, and continuity across administrations are design abstractions. They do not establish the actual constitutional framework of any starting nation. Country-specific powers, case procedures, and succession arrangements require dated primary evidence and appropriate historical analysis.

## Research still required

- Dated starting profiles for every selected nation, including period-specific monetary mandates and legal powers.
- Comparable economic, fiscal, sectoral, demographic, and distributional data.
- Evidence on ownership reforms, competition, public provision, planning systems, and institutional capacity.
- Policy lags, uncertainty, distributional effects, and variation across national circumstances.
- Historical shocks and a rule for separating external events from policy-dependent developments.
- Separate research for any 1980s scenario.
- Corporate and banking structures, supervisory authority, trade commitments, and enforcement institutions for each country and starting year.
- Empirical evidence for actor responses to regulation, credit conditions, industrial support, and trade changes, including conflicting findings and uncertainty.
- Formal and effective executive powers, legislative procedures, budget fallback rules, judicial review, and succession arrangements for each starting nation.
- The historical mix of fixed and variable borrowing, maturities, refinancing, deposit pricing, and monetary-policy transmission across household and enterprise groups.

For each profile or mechanism, record the observation period, definitions, source, simplification, and uncertainty. Do not treat a contemporary institutional page as sufficient evidence of its exact 2010 rules.
