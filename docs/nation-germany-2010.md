## Status, time boundary and player briefing

This is Germany's contribution to the accepted [six-nation research comparison]({{ '/nation-research/' | relative_url }}). The institutional opening is **1 January 2010**, an editorial convention for comparing profiles, not a selected gameplay starting date. No sector or first playable nation is selected. This document supplies researched structures and candidate choices; it does **not** supply a calibrated or runnable baseline.

Carbon-emissions and climate-change challenges, policies, objectives and scoring are outside the game. Historical publications sometimes discuss them; their omission here is a model boundary, not a claim that they were absent historically. No combined historical estimate is repurposed as a coefficient for the remaining policies.

**Proposed player briefing:** You lead the federal cabinet. Your agenda must pass through coalition politics, state governments, independent adjudication and European institutions. Public ownership is significant in some activities, while many investment and employment decisions belong to enterprises. A workable national programme needs both a funding route and an institution that can deliver it. Germany offers a strong teaching case for the difference between proposing a policy and controlling every actor needed to implement it.

At the opening, Angela Merkel had begun her second chancellorship on 28 October 2009. The 2009 election returned CDU 194 seats, CSU 45 and FDP 93: **332 of 622 seats** combined, calculated from the official result. That is an initial parliamentary position, not a guarantee that every future proposal passes. [DE01–DE02](#evidence-register)

## Government and authority

The following is an institutional map, not a complete legal opinion for a particular bill. Constitutional provisions cited from a current consolidation are limited to historically applicable arrangements; DE03's amendment history and period sources provide the checks. Fiscal, judicial and federal-financing articles have subsequently changed, so the current constitution must never be imported wholesale into the scenario.

| Policy area | Executive route at the opening | Other authority and practical limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spending and tax | Propose a federal budget and legislation; administer authorized programmes | Bundestag authorization; Bundesrat consent where constitutionally required, notably relevant shared-tax legislation; separate Land budgets. DE03 |
| Ownership | Exercise federal shareholder rights; propose lawful acquisitions or ownership legislation | Property protection and compensation rules; separate Land/municipal owners. Owning a company does not erase its corporate governance. DE03, DE10 |
| Regulation and competition | Propose statutory changes and use valid delegated authority | Sector regulators, competition enforcement, courts and applicable EU rules remain distinct. Railway access was regulated by BNetzA and EBA in 2009. DE10, DE15 |
| Central banking | Communicate and participate in European policymaking | Independent Eurosystem monetary decisions; no cabinet-controlled German policy rate. DE05–DE06 |
| Trade | Seek changes through EU institutions | EU customs union and common commercial policy are exclusive Union competences. Germany cannot independently set its own external tariff schedule. DE05 |
| Education and training | Set the federal in-company training framework and negotiate support | Länder control school education; employers provide training places; chambers and social partners participate. DE09 |

**Continuity:** the Bundestag elects the Chancellor; replacement through constructive no confidence requires electing a successor. Ordinary Bundestag terms are four years, subject to constitutional exceptions. The Bundesrat represents Land governments, not a second nationwide electorate. Article 111 permits specified essential and existing-obligation spending before a budget law; it does not authorize any new programme the cabinet wishes. DE03.

This fallback matters immediately: the 2010 federal budget was adopted in March, according to the Bundesbank's later account. A January opening must therefore distinguish provisional authority from the subsequently adopted budget. DE04. The exact provisional spending instructions and commitment ceilings remain a dependent research item.

The Federal Constitutional Court supplies evidence of a substantive limit rather than an abstract approval score. On **9 February 2010**, it found the method underlying standard subsistence benefits constitutionally deficient and required replacement rules by year end, while temporarily preserving the existing provisions. It did not simply choose a new benefit amount. This is **later-2010 evidence of practical review**, not a ruling already known on 1 January or an inevitable event in a counterfactual campaign. DE08.

For design, coalition bargaining and Land interests should have separate explanations. A Land may oppose an unfunded delivery obligation even when one of its governing parties supports the federal coalition. That is a proposed actor motive, not a measured likelihood. A usable voting model still needs the dated Bundesrat coalition composition and a bill-specific consent/objection classification; neither is invented here.

## Ownership, services and household circumstances

### Enterprise decisions and public provision

Deutsche Bahn AG's 2009 statements identify the Federal Republic as its sole shareholder. They also describe a corporate management and supervisory-board structure and regulated infrastructure access. This establishes a concrete combination of **public ownership, corporate organization and competition rules**. It does not establish that the cabinet can directly set every timetable, investment or supplier contract. DE10.

For private firms, production and investment should be represented as enterprise decisions subject to finance, demand and regulation. The evidence here supports broad manufacturing and service groupings, not a complete company roster. Before naming a private corporation as a persistent game actor, obtain its 2009 ownership, activities, geographic exposure and financial statements. Do not assign every German firm an export strategy or the same bargaining power.

**Healthcare:** compulsory coverage through statutory or private insurance had been established by 2009. The WHO-hosted system review distinguishes insurance financing from provision and describes shared federal, Land and self-governing responsibilities. This is retrospective institutional evidence; its 2014 provider counts and spending levels are not opening values. The game should distinguish a financing reform from hiring medical staff or expanding facilities. Exact 2010 eligibility, contributions and provider-payment rules remain necessary before modelling household charges. DE11.

**Education and skills:** the period BIBB report explains a dual training arrangement: workplace training and vocational schooling, with different responsibilities. Länder handle the school side; firms, federal rules, chambers and social partners shape workplace training. An appropriation alone therefore cannot create a cohort of qualified workers. This is a delivery distinction, not an estimated training-effect coefficient. DE09.

**Income protection and housing:** the court's account of the existing SGB II system describes means-tested support for eligible working-age people and their households, including accommodation and heating costs. Keep this separate from contributory unemployment insurance and pensions. The OECD's period survey also describes a substantial rental sector and a comparatively low owner-occupation share; it does not justify assuming that all households face mortgage-rate changes. DE08, DE12.

For a distributional model, proposed household groups should distinguish renters, mortgagors and outright owners; employed people, unemployed claimants and pension recipients; families with care responsibilities; and liquid savers versus borrowers. These are **proposed analytical groups**, not population shares or fixed political attitudes. Eligibility, income and asset distributions must be supplied by dated evidence before groups receive weights.

### Production, infrastructure, trade and regions

The Bundesbank's 2009 account describes the severe downturn and the importance of the external-demand shock. A reasonable opening briefing can explain exposure to foreign orders without telling the player that the actual 2010 recovery is guaranteed. DE13. Railway infrastructure and transport businesses are concrete interconnected assets in the DB report; spending on one should not be equated automatically with usable service capacity. DE10.

Retrospective goods-trade totals show the scale of cross-border exchange: 2010 exports were €951,959 million and imports €797,097 million in the Destatis table retrieved for this profile. These are goods-trade values, not domestic value added or the whole goods-and-services balance. The source's current historical series must be labelled by its retrieval vintage. DE14.

The period OECD survey documents continuing eastern/western differences in income, industrial structure and population movements. It also describes supplier links with foreign production locations. These support regional and intermediate-input distinctions, not a permanent national productivity bonus. DE12. A Germany-wide household or employment average should not stand in for each Land or for local housing conditions.

Population ageing was an identified planning issue: Destatis's twelfth coordinated projection was presented in November 2009. Its alternative fertility, longevity and migration assumptions are **projections**, not future population facts to enforce during play. DE16. The profile does not yet extract an opening age distribution or reconcile the later census-related revisions.

Fuel, food and component dependencies need product-and-partner tables with an observation year. This packet does not assign a Russian-gas percentage, self-sufficiency score or import-substitution capacity from present-day impressions. Those gaps block a quantified supply-disruption mechanic, not the broad institutional comparison.

## Money, banks and public finances

### The monetary arrangement

Germany used the euro. Euro-area monetary policy belongs to the Eurosystem; Treaty Article 130 protects the ECB and national central banks against government instructions. At the end of 2009 the ECB main-refinancing rate was **1.00%**, with deposit and marginal-lending facilities at **0.25%** and **1.75%**. These are central-bank instrument rates, not mortgage rates or Germany's government-bond yield. DE05–DE06.

The design consequence is to separate euro-area monetary conditions from German fiscal policy, individual bank offers and borrower contracts. A German investment initiative must not mechanically cause a German rate decision. Contract maturity and credit risk remain necessary even when the currency is shared.

Bank supervision in the opening arrangement involved **BaFin and the Bundesbank**. The Bundesbank's period report documents operational supervision and BaFin-ordered inspections. DE13. Its following annual report dates the new European supervisory authorities and German Restructuring Act to **2011**. These are not opening tools; still later banking-union arrangements also require a separate dated research packet. DE17.

### Different lenders and ownership routes

The OECD describes private commercial, public-sector and cooperative banking pillars, with savings banks and Landesbanken differing in ownership and activity. It identifies serious Landesbank problems in the financial crisis. This is evidence against treating either public ownership or private ownership as an automatic guarantee of prudent lending. DE12.

Deutsche Bank provides a private commercial-bank example: its 2009 annual report documents listed shares and both corporate/investment and private-client activities. Its shareholder and business structure should not be replaced with a fictional public mandate. The report is issuer evidence, not proof of future resilience. DE19.

KfW's 2009 issuer filing provides a separate example: a promotional institution whose capital was held **80% by the Federation and 20% by the Länder** as of March 2009. DE07. Its promotional role offers a possible policy channel, but a selected programme still needs its applicable mandate, funding, intermediary arrangement and risk allocation researched. A public guarantee is a contingent obligation, not free productive capacity.

### Borrowing and the 2009 constitutional reform

The debt brake was enacted in 2009 but did **not** impose the fully phased-in federal structural limit on the 2010 budget. Application began in 2011, with the federal transition ending in 2016 and the original Land transition in 2020. The 0.35%-of-GDP federal structural borrowing ceiling belongs to that dated framework, including cyclical treatment and emergency provisions. The earlier investment-linked rule remained relevant to 2010. DE03–DE04. Do not substitute later constitutional revisions or a universal debt threshold.

The federal borrowing report for 2009 distinguishes instrument types and maturities. It even records a **US$4 billion three-year issue** in September 2009 whose federal interest and currency risks were fully hedged. Thus neither “all debt is euro denominated” nor “foreign-currency issuance equals unhedged currency exposure” is safe. DE18.

A first model must initialize federal debt separately from consolidated general-government debt, social-insurance accounts and bank-support obligations. Remaining maturities, coupons and hedges have not been extracted into reconciled buckets here. Existing fixed obligations must not all reprice when market yields change. The historical source supplies a route to that work, not permission to invent a maturity distribution.

## Candidate policy families

These are **research-supported possibilities**, not selected mechanics. Motives and consequences below are hypotheses for later testing; no magnitude, probability or outcome is claimed.

| Candidate choice | Route and actors | Tradeoff to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change tax relief or the pace of public investment | Federal legislation and appropriations; affected Länder and delivery bodies; dated debt-rule transition | Households or firms may gain spending room, while revenue and implementation capacity constrain scale. Compare immediate relief with delayed construction and a no-change baseline. |
| Expand apprenticeship support or adult retraining | Federal training/benefit authority within its remit; Land schools, employers, chambers and unions (DE09) | Employers may value future skills but resist training costs during weak demand; trainees need suitable places and income. A subsidy could add training or pay for places that would already exist. |
| Reform household support or health financing | Amend the relevant social legislation and funding; insurers, providers, claimants and courts (DE08, DE11) | Better coverage may reduce insecurity, but administrative eligibility and provider capacity matter. Lower public cost could shift costs to households; legal adequacy is not an optional popularity penalty. |
| Use promotional finance or restructure a public lender | A programme-specific KfW route or the actual bank owner's route; supervision and EU state-aid review (DE07, DE15) | Credit support may preserve viable investment or prolong losses. Owners, creditors, borrowers and taxpayers have different exposures. Establish loss sharing before allowing an offer. |
| Change rail investment or access arrangements | Federal ownership/budget powers, company governance, regulators and applicable European rules (DE10, DE15) | Capacity investment may improve service after construction; access reform may encourage entry while complicating coordination. Neither privatization nor continued public ownership receives an automatic efficiency bonus. |

The EU state-aid framework matters particularly for selective support: Article 107 sets a prohibition with specified compatibility routes, rather than a claim that every subsidy is always forbidden. DE15. Each candidate must be reviewed against the measure's actual classification.

## What must be researched before a playable model

1. **A legislative route:** retrieve the 1 January Bundesrat composition, actual provisional-budget instructions, dated statutory versions and the chosen bill's consent requirements. This blocks a faithful authorization/voting mechanic, not the profile comparison.
2. **Reconciled fiscal and financial accounts:** extract end-2009 federal debt by remaining maturity, interest treatment and hedge exposure; reconcile Land/local/social-insurance accounts and crisis support. This blocks refinancing and fiscal-space calculations.
3. **The chosen policy's delivery chain:** for training, count eligible firms, instructors and places; for health, specify insurer/provider payment and capacity; for rail, define projects, ownership, access and procurement obligations. No sector is selected by this list.
4. **Weighted household and regional groups:** retrieve dated income, tenure, debt, age and employment distributions, documenting census revisions and Berlin's treatment in east/west comparisons. This blocks quantified welfare and regional effects.
5. **Conditional response evidence:** identify programme evaluations and bank/firm behaviour estimates relevant to the selected intervention. Historical annual changes alone cannot establish policy effectiveness. Register uncertainty and sensitivity ranges before selecting coefficients.

**Preferred next research step:** use the shared nation comparison to choose one first playable nation, then bound its initial policy choices. **Alternative:** close Germany's authorization and fiscal-data gaps first if federal bargaining is the user's preferred learning focus. Neither option reopens the accepted six-nation research or authorizes game implementation.
