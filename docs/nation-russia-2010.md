## Status, time boundary and player briefing

This profile contributes to the accepted [six-nation comparison]({{ '/nation-research/' | relative_url }}). **1 January 2010** is an editorial institutional snapshot, not a selected gameplay starting date. No first nation or policy sector is selected. This is a researched account with candidate choices and explicit gaps, not a calibrated or runnable baseline.

Carbon-emissions and climate-change challenges, policies, events and scoring are outside the game. Their discussion in historical sources does not bring them into the simulation. Production, trade and infrastructure matter here through employment, prices, revenues, ownership, financing and delivery.

**Proposed player briefing:** You direct a federal economic programme in a system with a powerful presidency, a separate government, regional administrations and substantial public influence over important enterprises. Private firms and households still make consequential decisions. The financial crisis has disrupted demand and credit. Your task is to choose which commitments to protect, how to finance them and which institutions can actually deliver the programme.

Dmitry Medvedev was President and Vladimir Putin was Prime Minister at the opening. Putin's appointment followed Medvedev's 2008 election; treating Putin as the 2010 President would misidentify the legal office. The 2007 election had returned **315 of 450 Duma seats** to United Russia. These dated facts are documented retrospectively by PACE; subsequent elections are not opening information. [RU02](#evidence-register)

The constitutional reference is explicitly the text amended through **30 December 2008**. Present-day constitutional arrangements must not replace it. Later outcomes in this document describe the historical record, not events that a counterfactual campaign must reproduce. RU01.

## Government and authority

The President and government are separate actors. The President appoints the Prime Minister with Duma consent and can dismiss the government; the government prepares and executes the federal budget and manages federal property. Presidential decrees and government orders operate within the constitutional and statutory hierarchy. RU01.

| Policy area | Route for the federal executive | Other authority or implementation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spending and tax | Government submits budget and tax legislation; implements authorized spending | Duma legislation, Federation Council consideration and presidential enactment; regional budgets remain separate. RU01 |
| Ownership | Exercise federal property and shareholder powers; seek legislation for broader changes | Corporate governance, ownership rights and separate municipal/regional assets; state influence is not a single legal ownership category. RU01, RU03 |
| Regulation and competition | Propose laws and use delegated administrative powers | Competition enforcement, sector rules and courts; implementation capacity needs separate evidence. RU03 |
| Central banking | Coordinate economic policy through the lawful institutional relationship | CBR's constitutional currency function and operational monetary decisions; bank supervision is distinct from a ministry's industrial support programme. RU01, RU04 |
| Trade | Negotiate and implement external economic arrangements | The new customs union constrains a purely national tariff choice; WTO membership has not yet begun. RU07–RU08 |
| Services and regional delivery | Establish federal frameworks, finance programmes and negotiate transfers | Regions, municipalities, insurance funds and service providers deliver different parts; a federal announcement does not itself supply staff or facilities. RU01, RU06 |

Local self-government was constitutionally separate from the system of state authorities within its competence. That formal protection does not demonstrate financial independence; municipal revenues and transfer conditions still need dated evidence. RU01, Article 12.

**Continuity and succession:** Article 92 makes the Prime Minister acting President if the President cannot perform the role, with restrictions on that temporary office. Article 117 specifies presidential choices following government resignation or Duma no confidence; no confidence is not automatically an immediate change of executive. The 2008 amendments lengthened future presidential and Duma terms, without extending the terms of the incumbents elected before those amendments. RU01.

**Budget fallback is an identified legal research dependency.** Budget Code Article 190 concerns temporary budget management when a budget has not entered into force. The WTO-hosted translation supplies a candidate legal text, but its complete amendment cutoff and precise applicability at the opening have not been established here. Do not implement an unrestricted continuation of spending, an automatic shutdown or an exact monthly ceiling from this unfinished verification. Obtain the operative 2010 budget law, Article 190 version and execution instructions before a budget-delay mechanic is approved. RU09.

### Formal institutions and effective power

Constitutional federalism does not imply independently elected governors at this date. PACE's historical account records the 2004 replacement of direct gubernatorial elections by presidential appointment with regional-assembly consent. Its assessment connects this arrangement and the governing party's Duma majority with stronger executive power. Those are dated institutional findings; the report's later discussion of 2012 reforms must not be backdated. RU02.

The Constitution provides independent courts and constitutional review. Formal text alone cannot establish how reliably a politically important company or citizen could secure a remedy in 2010. PACE's later monitoring report discusses judicial independence, but its broad 2012 assessment is not used as a numerical 2010 constraint. A playable enforcement model needs period cases, implementation evidence and an explicit scope, rather than an invented national corruption or judicial-obedience score. RU01–RU02.

For proposed gameplay, preserve the presidency, government, governing party, regional authorities and enterprise managers as distinguishable actors. A large parliamentary majority may make legislation easier while leaving disagreements over funding, appointments and delivery. The relative personal influence of Medvedev and Putin is **not quantified by this evidence**; do not turn their formal titles into a fabricated power ratio.

## Ownership, services and households

### Enterprises and competition

The period OECD survey describes private activity alongside state-controlled enterprises and special state corporations. It warns that enterprises owned by state-owned enterprises may be classified statistically as private. RU03. For design, a company count or one ownership column is therefore insufficient: record direct ownership, indirect control and the decisions each confers separately.

The CBR's 2009 report provides a concrete bank example: it held **57.58% of Sberbank's capital**, with a larger share of voting rights. Its shareholder role must be separated from its regulatory and monetary roles. A decision to support lending, an instruction to change banking rules and a shareholder decision are different actions with different routes. RU04.

Gazprom and Rosneft illustrate the OECD ownership-classification warning. RU03. Their precise January 2010 ownership chains still require verification. This profile supplies neither an audited company roster nor a universal state-enterprise objective.

**Proposed actor motives:** managers could seek investment funding and organizational survival; private entrants could seek access to customers and fair procurement; workers could seek reliable wages and continued employment. Public owners could value dividends, strategic capacity or employment. These are explanatory hypotheses, not evidence that all actors in a category behave alike.

### Healthcare, education, housing and social protection

The health system combined inherited public service infrastructure with mandatory health insurance introduced in 1993. The European Observatory's 2011 review describes mixed budget and insurance financing and substantial urban–rural differences in access. It also discusses reforms then under way: their later payment and financing arrangements cannot all be treated as existing on 1 January 2010. RU06.

A useful healthcare choice therefore distinguishes entitlement, funding, purchasing and provision. Extra funding might relieve a local bottleneck, but it does not establish how many appointments or treatments become available. Before simulating health outcomes, obtain the dated division of obligations, regional facility and workforce data, insurance coverage definitions and the effective dates of the 2010–2011 changes.

Education has constitutional guarantees concerning free access through public institutions and competitive access to publicly funded higher education. Housing provisions include support for construction and specified assistance for eligible households; they do not promise a free dwelling to every household. These are legal entitlements, not measurements of school quality, waiting lists or available homes. RU01, Articles 40 and 43.

The practical education and housing baselines remain incomplete. A school programme needs the 2010 federal/regional/municipal financing split and enrolment or capacity data. A housing programme needs tenure, municipal stock, eligibility, maintenance obligations and regional construction data. Those gaps prevent assigning a national service-quality score from constitutional promises.

Pensions are a distinct fiscal and household channel. The IMF's August 2010 assessment describes substantial pension increases supporting consumption during recovery. This is later evidence, not proof that the whole reported increase was effective at the opening. Pensioners, wage earners, unemployed households and households servicing loans should have separate exposure to policy changes. RU05.

Regional conditions should likewise remain explicit. The health review supports urban–rural access differences; it does not justify assigning the same shortage to every rural area. The opening age structure, working-age population, regional employment and migration data have not been extracted. Population totals alone cannot supply dependency ratios, pension caseloads or labour supply.

## Production, infrastructure and external exposure

The financial crisis is central to the opening economic context. The IMF's 2010 assessment describes the interaction of falling oil prices, capital outflows and the reversal of the preceding credit boom, with recovery beginning during the second half of 2009. It also describes weak lending amid balance-sheet repair. These mechanisms support considering export revenue, private finance and domestic demand together; they do not establish a fixed response coefficient. RU05.

Production should include resource extraction, manufacturing, construction, agriculture and services once comparable dated accounts are assembled. Infrastructure candidates include transport, housing services and communications as well as electricity. The first playable sector is not selected here. A maintenance programme, for example, would require identification of the asset owner, procurement route, capital requirement, operating budget and available suppliers before a service improvement could be calculated.

The **Russia–Belarus–Kazakhstan customs union began its common-tariff phase in January 2010**. Implementation was a process, rather than an assumption that all internal border arrangements disappeared on the first day. Russia joined the WTO only on **22 August 2012**. Neither later WTO rights and commitments nor the later Eurasian Economic Union should be imported into the opening. RU07–RU08.

For a proposed trade policy, distinguish an executive negotiating position from an immediately available national tariff button. A producer may want protection while an importer or household wants cheaper inputs or goods. Employment, consumer prices, customs revenue and partner responses are possible consequences to investigate, not preassigned effects.

## Money, banks and public finances

The opening monetary regime was a **managed float**, using a dollar/euro basket and foreign-exchange intervention. The basket had comprised **0.55 US dollars and 0.45 euros** since February 2007. CBR's history distinguishes the moving operational band from outer fixed bounds, removed only in October 2010. A free-floating, fully developed inflation-targeting regime is therefore the wrong opening description. RU10.

This creates a proposed policy tension: exchange-rate stabilization, domestic credit conditions and reserve use interact. It does not mean the player can independently choose any exchange rate, interest rate and reserve balance. Neither a modern policy-rate label nor a universal debt-to-interest formula should be substituted for the dated instruments and market relationships.

CBR's period annual report documents banking supervision and crisis responses. The present-day financial mega-regulator structure must not be assumed for 2010. Publicly influenced banks, private domestic banks and foreign-owned banks need separate balance sheets and incentives; this profile establishes the distinction, while leaving a verified named private-bank case outstanding. RU04.

The OECD describes pre-crisis foreign-currency borrowing funding ruble exposures. RU03. A proposed financing model should consequently distinguish borrowers, importers and exporters, check hedging, and keep sovereign debt, bank liabilities and corporate debt separate. No universal unhedged position is assumed.

The period IMF assessment supplies fiscal and external tables and records the fiscal response to the downturn. A deficit is not the same as an immediate inability to finance expenditure. However, this profile does not yet reconcile the federal budget with consolidated government and social funds, or extract debt by currency, holder and maturity. Before financing choices become playable, distinguish tax receipts, fund drawdowns, borrowing, guarantees and enterprise support. Central-bank reserves cannot simply be counted as freely spendable federal revenue. RU04–RU05.

## Candidate policy families

These are research-supported possibilities, **not selected mechanics or simulated results**.

| Candidate | Route and actors | Proposed motives and consequences to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adjust pension or targeted household support | Government budget/benefit proposals, legislature, administering funds and regions | Households seek dependable income; finance officials seek durable funding. Consumption support and recurring obligations need separate evaluation. RU01, RU05 |
| Improve competition and public procurement | Legislation, enforcement agencies, purchasing bodies and courts | Entrants seek access; incumbents defend contracts; administrators need deliverable services. Lower prices or reduced favouritism are hypotheses requiring sector evidence. RU03 |
| Change state-enterprise investment or ownership | Lawful shareholder decisions, company governance, budget/privatization authority | Public owners may seek investment or receipts; managers and employees may resist disruption. Sale proceeds, dividends and service obligations are separate flows. RU03–RU04 |
| Repair service infrastructure | Budget appropriation, intergovernmental funding and responsible asset owners | Regions seek reliable services; contractors seek paid work; households care about cost and access. Capacity and maintenance evidence must precede benefits. RU01, RU06 |
| Support bank repair and credit access | Distinct fiscal-support and CBR supervisory/monetary routes | Banks seek solvency and funding; firms seek working capital; public authorities face contingent exposure. More support does not guarantee more productive lending. RU04–RU05 |

The legal route must be checked again for the specific instrument chosen. These candidates do not authorize a generic executive override of courts, company law or the customs arrangement.

## Specific blockers before a playable baseline

1. **Executive and legislative actions:** select the actual player office; obtain period procedural law and instrument-specific authority, plus dated evidence of implementation and review. Formal presidency and premiership must remain distinguishable.
2. **Budgets and household support:** verify the 2010 budget, temporary-budget provisions, pension effective dates, intergovernmental transfers and programme eligibility; reconcile federal and general-government accounts before any affordability test.
3. **Finance:** extract opening monetary instruments, bank exposures and sovereign/corporate debt separately by currency and maturity; verify a named private-bank example. This blocks calibrated lending, exchange-rate and refinancing consequences.
4. **Ownership and services:** build dated direct/indirect ownership records and service delivery maps. Resolve education, housing and regional health responsibilities before assigning costs or quality effects.
5. **Production and households:** obtain sector accounts, imports/exports, age and household distributions and regional employment; document revisions and permissible reuse. These block production, labour, price and distributional calculations.

**Preferred next step:** compare these authority and delivery differences with the other five profiles, then choose the first nation and a bounded policy family. The alternative is one focused evidence pass addressing a named blocker above. Neither path implies that coding or a complete historical simulation has already been authorized.
