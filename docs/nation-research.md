## Accepted research scope

On 25 September 2026 the user selected broad **2010 profiles for all six nations**, with one researcher assigned to each nation in two batches. Execution uses three worker threads, each handling one nation at a time and a different nation in the second batch. The research precedes selection of a first playable nation and policy family. It has no electricity, housing or other sector prerequisite. Carbon-emissions and climate-change challenges remain excluded throughout the game.

This packet, **P2a**, establishes descriptive profiles, authority routes and evidence gaps. **P2b** will collect the detailed starting data for whichever nation and policy scope the user later selects. Neither a profile nor the comparison below is a calibrated model, a playable nation pack, or authorization to implement six campaigns.

## Shared time and evidence conventions

**Institutional reference: 1 January 2010.** This is an editorial convention for a consistent comparison, not a user selection of the exact campaign opening date. Later events within 2010 are labeled separately. A law announced or enacted later in the year cannot supply an opening power; enactment, commencement and actual administrative operation must be distinguished.

Three dates answer different questions: **observation date** says when something happened; **publication date** says when it could be known; **retrieval or revision date** identifies the version checked. An annual report published in 2011 can document the 2010 economy without becoming information available to a player on 1 January 2010. Even a retrospective estimate of 2009 may contain revisions unavailable at the opening.

Prefer period legislation, budgets, institutional reports and statistical releases. Current explanatory pages may support a durable institutional principle only when its historical applicability is checked. Formal legal powers and observed practice are separate evidence categories. A constitution alone cannot establish the effective independence of a court, the probability of a bill passing, or the reliability of local implementation.

Each profile covers executive and legislative authority, courts, regional roles, ownership and competition, public services, households, production and trade, money and banking, public finances, and several possible reforms. Actor motives and possible gameplay consequences are design interpretations unless tied to specific evidence. No profile assigns permanent national efficiency, ideological success bonuses or invented behavioral coefficients.

## What the comparison must preserve

A public shareholding, a bank guarantee, prudential supervision and monetary policy are different powers. A government may hold shares while a firm's board makes operating decisions; a regulator may constrain private firms without owning them. Profiles therefore describe ownership and authority separately, including the mixed arrangements visible during the financial crisis.

Likewise, central budget approval does not necessarily authorize regional delivery or a particular loan. Each nation needs its own route through legislation, administration, courts and subnational bodies. A budget delay must follow that nation's actual funding rules, not a universal shutdown mechanic. Where a profile cannot yet certify the exact fallback or legal remedy, the dependent gameplay branch remains unspecified.

The comparison should also preserve households' different exposure to employment, housing costs, services and debt. Survey periods, geographical coverage and definitions differ. England-only housing observations are not UK totals, a national poverty threshold is not an internationally uniform poverty measure, and a census-day headcount is not necessarily the WDI midyear population estimate. These are reasons to label and reconcile evidence before assigning model weights.

This is a synthesis of the linked profiles and their evidence registers. The final column is a **design interpretation**, not a policy recommendation, national ranking or selected feature. Exact authority remains instrument-specific.

| [Russia]({{ '/nation-russia-2010/' | relative_url }}) | Presidency and government remain distinct; constitutional powers and observed practice need separate evidence. | [China]({{ '/nation-china-2010/' | relative_url }}) | Party and state offices, national and local budgets, public owners and commercial firms must remain distinguishable. | [Japan]({{ '/nation-japan-2010/' | relative_url }}) | The Diet gives budgets and ordinary bills different disagreement procedures; budget approval does not automatically pass a separate financing law. The current evidence is uneven. Some profiles have a documented budget-continuity framework; Russia's exact operative fallback and Japan's consolidated 2010 provisional-budget rules still need legal-version checks. Some service statistics cover only one jurisdiction or a later survey period. These differences are visible research dependencies, not invented differences in national competence. The broad research packet can be complete while a particular nation-policy model remains blocked on those inputs.

## From research to a playable choice

A useful first nation is one whose chosen policy can be explained through a bounded set of documented institutions and measurable outcomes. That is an engineering and teaching judgment, not a claim that its economy or politics is objectively simpler or superior.

After comparing the profiles, choose one nation, then one policy family and the player's objective. Only then collect the transaction-level or aggregate starting data needed by the actual rules: spending authority, funding, affected households and firms, physical capacity, delivery delays, loan and debt terms, and a no-change alternative. Research gaps in each profile name which features depend on further evidence.

Broad profiles do not require a sector focus. Precise policy simulations do: a housing rule needs evidence about land, tenure, construction and lending; a tax-and-transfer rule needs coverage, income groups, collection and eligibility. Selecting that detail later avoids treating one sector as the defining feature of every nation.

## Review and acceptance boundary

The parent researcher integrates the independently authored profiles, checks temporal consistency and source coverage, and compares institutional routes. Missing or inaccessible evidence stays visible. Documentation review can establish that claims have support and that limits are disclosed; it cannot establish behavioral calibration, forecast accuracy or device usability.

For this packet, each author reviewed its sources and date boundaries. Parent review covered all six texts, corrected source-table and anchor issues, checked the shared statistics against the saved extract, and independently spot-checked consequential primary sources: the UK December 2009 monetary decision, US GMAC ownership announcement, Germany's debt-rule transition, Russia's historical constitution and exchange regime, and Japan's monetary decision and constitutional routes. China's source provenance and executive/Party role boundary received an additional review. This is a documented research review, not independent replication of every cited dataset or certification of every possible policy route.

Publication checks cover page rendering, navigation, anchors, assets and source links. The [backlog]({{ '/backlog/' | relative_url }}) and handover record actual completion evidence. No game implementation, synthetic model experiment or phone test is claimed by this research packet.
